Gukesh vs Sindarov: World Chess Championship 2026 begins with a historic title clash

India’s reigning champion faces Uzbekistan’s Candidates winner in Geneva, with Black in the opening game.

By Chessroom · 24 November 2026

The 2026 World Chess Championship began on November 24 in Geneva, Switzerland, with India's D Gukesh facing Uzbekistan's Javokhir Sindarov. Both grandmasters are 20 years old, making this the youngest pairing in the history of the championship.

Sindarov opened Game 1 with White, while defending champion Gukesh took the black pieces. The opening encounter was still underway in the reports available at the time of writing.

What happened in Game 1?

Sindarov began with 1.e4 and Gukesh answered with 1...e5. The players entered the Berlin Defence of the Ruy Lopez, exchanging queens on move eight. Despite the early queen exchange, the position offered plenty of strategic decisions.

According to the Times of India’s 9:28 PM IST update, Gukesh had used more than 30 minutes considering his response to 17.b3. Sindarov had gained an early advantage on the clock, but no final result had been reported in that update.

Gukesh and Sindarov: two different paths to Geneva

D Gukesh — defending world champion

Gukesh took the title from Ding Liren in Singapore in 2024 at age 18. His recent tournament results have been less consistent, and he has cut back on competition to prepare for this match.

Javokhir Sindarov — the challenger

Sindarov qualified by winning the 2026 Candidates Tournament without a defeat. He also contributed to Uzbekistan’s team victory at the Chess Olympiad in Samarkand.

World Chess Championship 2026 format and schedule

The match comprises up to 14 classical games. A win earns one point and a draw earns half a point. The first player to reach 7.5 points takes the title. If the classical portion ends 7–7, faster games will decide the championship.

Where to follow Gukesh vs Sindarov

Fans can follow the games through the official FIDE championship website or the World Chess match centre. The colours alternate for each classical game, with Gukesh scheduled to play White in Game 2.
