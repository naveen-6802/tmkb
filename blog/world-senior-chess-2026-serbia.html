World Senior Chess 2026 opens in Serbia with 385 players

Vrnjačka Banja welcomes an international field for four senior world titles.

By Chessroom · 3 November 2026

The 2026 World Senior Chess Championship opened in Vrnjačka Banja, Serbia, on 3 November. A field of 385 players representing 65 national federations has gathered for the competition, including 44 grandmasters and eight women grandmasters.

Titles are contested in the 50+ and 65+ age groups, each with Open and Women’s sections. The event combines a substantial titled field with the opportunity for experienced players to compete within their own age categories.

A ceremonial first move at Hotel Zepter

The opening took place at Hotel Zepter before the first round, beginning with the Serbian and FIDE anthems. Serbian federation president Andrija Jorgić, FIDE technical delegate Gunnar Björnsson and municipal president Boban Đurović welcomed the participants.

Đurović and Jorgić then played the ceremonial first move. Chief arbiter Ashot Vardapetyan formally opened the competition, turning attention from the ceremony to the boards.

Four categories and a €50,000 prize fund

The total prize fund is €50,000. With separate competitions for the two age groups and the Open and Women’s categories, four world champions will emerge from the event.

Play continues through mid-November

The event’s arrival-to-departure dates run from 2 to 15 November. Following the opening day, the published programme includes a rest day on 8 November. The final round and closing ceremony are scheduled for 14 November, with departures the following day.

The classical time control gives players 90 minutes for the first 40 moves, followed by another 30 minutes for the remainder of the game. A 30-second increment applies from move one.

Vrnjačka Banja builds on its chess calendar

Hotel Zepter is the playing venue in a town already familiar to international chess visitors. Vrnjačka Banja hosted the European Individual Championship in 2023 and the European Club Cup events in 2024. Its recent calendar also includes the 2025 World School and World Amateur championships.

That background gives this year’s senior event a setting with an established connection to tournament chess, alongside the town’s identity as a Serbian spa destination.

Why this championship is worth following

Senior competitions offer a different perspective on a chess career. Age-group events give players a reason to keep preparing, travelling and testing their decisions long after their first competitive years. They also put experienced players in direct competition with peers who have spent decades studying the same game.

For spectators, classical games offer time to examine how a plan develops. Instead of watching only for a sudden tactical finish, it can be useful to follow a quieter decision: an exchange, a change in pawn structure or the choice to simplify into an endgame.

As the championship progresses, those individual decisions will build towards four title races. For now, the opening marks the start of a meeting between a large international field and a host town with a growing chess tradition.
